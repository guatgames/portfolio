# What Happens at Expo Kinal?

In this event the selected students have the opportunity to create their own project and exhibit it in front of people and many business owners. But this year there was a twist: it was the 65th anniversary of Kinal, so the projects presented had to be very useful, attractive and, above all, focused on Kinal.

## What changed this year?
This year, the students who wanted the opportunity to present their projects at Expo Kinal had to meet a series of requirements. This is not easy: out of more than 200 projects, only 56 were presented.

## What were the requirements?
1. First, the students needed to fill out a document describing the project, what problem it solves, how it is currently solved, how the project solves it, which technologies or objects it uses, and the team members.

2. After filling out the document, a coordinating committee in charge of Expo Kinal reviews it and decides whether the project lives up to Kinal's expectations.

3. If the project is approved, you and your team (if you have one) start working and have 4 weeks to finish the project and show it at Expo Kinal 2026.

## What did I do?
In this Expo Kinal, my team and I had to meet all the requirements. After filling out the document, our project was approved and became 1 of the 2 projects from the 5th degree of the Informatics evening program. Our project was a social network for KINAL students, built by KINAL students. It is an employment application where the companies that want to hire a KINAL student can view all registered students, offer jobs and post anything about the company. Users can post their skills, projects, technical area of study and course certificates.

Kinal-Connections is a social network and recruitment platform for the KINAL ecosystem; it connects students with companies seeking technical talent. Students post their profiles, skills, projects, technical areas, and certifications, while companies publish content, create job offers, and contact students.

## Why is this experience enriching?
This experience is very important to me because, by exposing myself to large crowds and being compelled to present the developed project to them, I gained the ability to communicate the project development process — how the projects work and how to explain them to people outside the world of IT. This experience also taught me the process of turning an idea into an approved and developed project. On this project we worked with a SCRUM framework, and I was the Scrum Master — the person in charge of organizing the team, assigning the work and deciding the course of the project.
